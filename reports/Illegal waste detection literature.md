# Verified Sources Anchor Zimbabwe's Smartphone Waste-Detection Dissertation

The dissertation stands on solid, checkable ground. **More than 120 sources** across the eight themes were checked against live Crossref records or official pages on 9 November 2026, and the literature consistently supports the project's central claim: **illegal-dumping detection research is dominated by aerial, satellite and UAV imagery, and no peer-reviewed study was found that applies ground-level, smartphone-based YOLO detection to dumping in Zimbabwe or anywhere in sub-Saharan Africa.** The closest African deep-learning work, Knoblauch et al. (2026), uses crowdsourced UAV imagery across 10 SSA countries ([Knoblauch et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compenvurbsys.2026.102517)). All 17 proposal references exist, but **seven carry citation-detail errors** (missing article numbers or pages, a wrong year, a wrong issue), including a broken DOI for Nwokediegwu et al. and a wrong first-author surname for Abou El-Magd et al. In addition, **ten of the 17 do not support a claim about smartphone or street-level dumping detection**: six concern overhead imagery, three concern classification or sorting, and one detects the act of littering in CCTV video. Two further sources come from venues an examiner may question (SCIRP and the Engineering Science & Technology Journal). Three findings should change how the dissertation is written. First, YOLO11 has been superseded by YOLO26 (January 2026), so the choice of model needs a sentence of justification. Second, published waste-detection mAP in cluttered real scenes clusters at 40–70%, which makes the project's reported box mAP@0.5 of about 0.40 defensible rather than embarrassing. Third, the W3C now defines the browser geolocation `accuracy` radius at 68% confidence, not 95%. The sections below give a themed Harvard bibliography with annotations and verification status, then cross-cutting agreements, contradictions and gaps, then the corrected 17 references.

## Reading the verification marks

Every entry carries a status. **Crossref** means the title, authors, year, venue, volume, pages and DOI were matched against the live Crossref API record on 9 November 2026. **Web** means the item was confirmed on the publisher's or issuer's own page. **Canonical** means a very widely cited record whose details come from the standard published record but was not re-fetched; click its DOI once before submission. **Partial** means the item exists but some details (pages, editor names, initials) were not confirmed. Figures marked *(secondary)* came from a citing paper, a search summary or a press report rather than the original full text, and should be checked against the paper before being quoted. Items the research could not confirm appear only in the "do not cite" list in the cross-cutting section. Lowercase DOIs are used throughout; DOIs are case-insensitive, so the uppercase forms in the proposal (for example "J.ECOINF") are harmless.

## Zimbabwe's waste crisis is well documented, but its numbers conflict

The context chapter has strong institutional and peer-reviewed support, but every headline figure comes in competing versions that must not be mixed. Sub-Saharan Africa's MSW collection rate is reported as **about 44%** for 2016 in *What a Waste 2.0* *(secondary, via press coverage)*, **31%** for 2022 on the current World Bank page ([World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/what-a-waste)), and **about 55%** Africa-wide around 2012 in UNEP's outlook, which also reports that **more than 90%** of waste went to uncontrolled dumpsites ([Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/un-csir-launch-africa-waste-management-outlook-2018-06-05)). These measure different regions, years and methods, so the dissertation should pick one and name its vintage. Harare's generation is likewise **762 t/day** in the UN-Habitat Waste Wise Cities assessment but "close to 1,000 t/day" in the *Herald*, against a fleet able to collect about 360 t/day ([Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/garbage-collection-overwhelms-council/)). In Bulawayo, the city council has identified **35 illegal dumpsites in the CBD**, linked to fleet breakdowns ([The Independent](https://theindependent.co.zw/local-news/article/200000414/bcc-identifies-35-illegal-dumpsites-in-cbd)). Fleet and capacity figures exist only in press reports, so cite them as grey literature.

The peer-reviewed Zimbabwean studies agree on causes. Kwenda et al. (2022) identify **low collection frequency and municipal incapacitation** among the leading causes of poor household waste management in Harare ([Kwenda et al.](https://doi.org/10.5276/jswtm/2022.375)). Sinthumule and Mkumbuzi (2019) surveyed 375 Nkulumane households and found that Bulawayo's community-based scheme, adopted in 2009, had **not changed disposal behaviour** ([Sinthumule and Mkumbuzi](https://doi.org/10.3390/resources8010030)). The health link needs careful wording. WHO's 2018 outbreak report attributes the Glen View/Budiriro cholera epidemic (**8,535 cases, 50 deaths** by 3 October, 98% in Harare) to contaminated wells and boreholes and **does not mention garbage** ([WHO](https://www.who.int/emergencies/disease-outbreak-news/item/05-october-2018-cholera-zimbabwe-en)). Ziraba et al. (2016) provide the framework linking poor waste management to disease, but the evidence they review is largely indirect ([Ziraba et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13690-016-0166-4)). The dissertation should therefore present illegal dumping as a **risk factor that co-occurs with** water and sanitation failure, not as a proven cause of cholera.

## Detection foundations justify a one-stage YOLO and an mAP@0.5 headline

The foundations literature traces a clear arc that the methodology chapter can follow. Detection moved from two-stage region-proposal detectors to one-stage dense detectors, then to transformers. Faster R-CNN ran at about 5 fps ([Ren et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01497)) while the original YOLO ran at 45 fps ([Redmon et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02640)). The accuracy gap between one-stage and two-stage detectors closed once FPN ([Lin et al., FPN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.03144)) and focal loss addressed foreground/background imbalance (RetinaNet at **39.1 AP** on COCO; [Lin et al., focal loss](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02002)). By 2024–2026 the field converged: YOLOv10 and YOLO26 adopted the NMS-free one-to-one matching introduced by DETR while keeping CNN efficiency. For a single-class detector meant to run behind a mobile reporting app, a one-stage YOLO is the efficient choice the literature supports.

The model choice needs one honest paragraph. Ultralytics released **YOLO26 in January 2026** with an official arXiv paper; YOLO26n reaches **40.9 mAP50-95** against YOLO11n's 39.5 and runs about 43% faster on CPU ([Ultralytics YOLO26](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yolo26/); [Jocher et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03748)). YOLO11 remains a mature, documented baseline ([Ultralytics YOLO11](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yolo11/)). The dissertation should acknowledge YOLO26 and either run it as a comparison or state why it was excluded, for example that the timeline was fixed before release. YOLO11 has **no peer-reviewed paper**, so cite it as software alongside the Khanam and Hussain preprint for its architecture (C3k2, SPPF, C2PSA) ([Khanam and Hussain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17725)).

On evaluation, PASCAL VOC fixed a true positive at IoU ≥ 0.5 ([Everingham et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11263-009-0275-4)), while COCO averages AP over IoU 0.50–0.95 and so rewards tight boxes ([Lin et al., COCO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0312)). Padilla et al. (2021) show that **AP implementations and annotation formats change reported scores**, so the toolkit (Ultralytics `val`) must be stated and raw numbers not compared across toolkits ([Padilla et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics10030279)). Waste piles have subjective extents, so mAP@0.5 is the fairer headline, with mAP@0.5:0.95 reported for comparability. No peer-reviewed source was found that formally treats image-level evaluation of detectors or the choice of a deployment confidence threshold. The student must therefore define image-level metrics ("at least one detection above τ") and select τ on the validation set before testing.

The small-data literature raises three issues the dissertation should address directly. First, the training set holds about **26% background images (971 of about 3,700)**, far above Ultralytics' informal "about 0–10%" guideline ([Ultralytics tips](https://docs.ultralytics.com/yolov5/tutorials/tips_for_best_training_results/)). No peer-reviewed study quantifies the optimal ratio, so an ablation or an explicit precision-first rationale is needed. Second, 800 of those negatives come from COCO. Torralba and Efros showed that classifiers learn dataset "signatures" ([Torralba and Efros](https://doi.org/10.1109/CVPR.2011.5995347)), so the model could learn "COCO style means no waste"; report results on a Zimbabwe-only test subset. Third, near-duplicate leakage inflates test scores: Barz and Denzler found duplicates in about 3.3% of CIFAR-10 and 10% of CIFAR-100 test images ([Barz and Denzler](https://doi.org/10.3390/jimaging6060041)). The dataset should be de-duplicated, and split by site or source, before the train/val/test split.

## Waste-detection studies split into 95%-plus classification and 40–70% real-world mAP

The waste-detection literature shows a two-tier pattern that the discussion chapter can exploit. Headline accuracies of **94–99%** come from classification on clean backgrounds (TrashNet), narrow container tasks (full-bin detection at 96%; [Abo-Zahhad and Abo-Zahhad](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-99885-x)), behaviour recognition (littering acts at up to 99.5%; [Alharbi et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11759333/)) or authors' own constrained datasets. Box-level detection in cluttered outdoor scenes is far lower. EcoDetect-YOLO reaches **58.1% mAP@0.5** and 31.1% mAP@0.5:0.95 on 3,995 surveillance images ([Liu et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11280945/)). Four detectors on the 13,000-image pLitterStreet set reach AP "above 40%" ([Mandhati et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14719)). Majchrowska et al. reach about 70% AP after merging public in-the-wild datasets ([Majchrowska et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06808)). Against that range, the project's reported box mAP@0.5 of about 0.40 sits at the low end, which is expected for amorphous dump piles. Its photo-level accuracy of about 93% is comparable to image-level figures: SpotGarbage's 87.69% on GINI *(secondary)* and Knoblauch et al.'s tile-level 92.87% *(secondary)*.

Domain sensitivity is the most important warning in this literature. Rajeev et al.'s YOLOv8 falls from **0.821 to 0.437 mAP@0.5** when moved from their 700-image e-waste set to the FICS-PCB benchmark ([Rajeev et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12104417/)). Only Moral et al. (eleven Spanish cities) and Knoblauch et al. (29 SSA regions) evaluate across multiple locations. No study was found that trains on European or Asian street litter and tests in Africa, yet that is effectively what fine-tuning on mixed Roboflow data and deploying in Harare does. Authors attribute low in-the-wild scores to small objects, long-tail imbalance, clutter, confusion between similar materials and dim or occluded targets ([Wu et al., RoLID-11K](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00398)). One further explanation applies specifically to piles and has not been tested in any source: a dump can validly be boxed as one region or several. A correct prediction can therefore fail IoU ≥ 0.5, which would explain low box mAP alongside high photo-level accuracy. This is a hypothesis the dissertation could test with an inter-annotator agreement study.

The dataset landscape confirms the novelty of the Zimbabwe set. Public waste datasets fall into four image domains: clean-background single objects (TrashNet, WaDaBa, MJU-Waste), in-the-wild ground litter (TACO, PlastOPol, GINI, pLitterStreet, RoLID-11K), conveyor belts (ZeroWaste) and aerial views (UAVVaste, AerialWaste, DroneWaste). **None of the ground-level sets comes from sub-Saharan Africa**, most label individual items rather than piles, and few include curated negative images ([waste-datasets-review](https://github.com/AgaMiko/waste-datasets-review)). The closest precedent is SpotGarbage (2016): a smartphone app with geotagged reports, a developing-country setting (India), garbage piles and negative images. It predates YOLO and should be cited prominently ([Mittal et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/2971648.2971731)).

## Aerial and satellite methods find old landfills, not fresh roadside dumps

Overhead detection is mature but structurally limited, and that limitation is the dissertation's opening. The best-documented result is Torres and Fraternali's ResNet50+FPN scene classifier at **88% precision and 87% recall** on 20 cm aerial imagery in Lombardy. The authors call on-site inspection "extremely costly and impossible to scale" ([Torres and Fraternali, 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13224520)). Two years later the same group stated that no public landfill dataset existed before AerialWaste ([Torres and Fraternali, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-01976-9)). Those results depend on 20 cm airborne or commercial very-high-resolution imagery (WorldView-3, Pléiades), which is costly and captured infrequently. Free Sentinel-2 (10 m) supports only susceptibility mapping: the Dakar study found **28–35%** of its area at high or very high susceptibility, combining Landsat and Sentinel indices with citizen-survey weights ([Scharf et al.](https://doi.org/10.3390/su172411137)).

The argument for complementarity rests on three gaps, but only the first is cited in the literature. First, revisit and latency: no verified abstract reports a revisit rate that would catch new dumping within days, whereas citizen reports arrive in near real time. Second, occlusion: overhead views miss waste under canopy, under bridges or in narrow lanes (an inference, not a measured finding). Third, scale: satellites find large, long-standing sites, while phones capture freshly dumped bags. African evidence is thin. It consists of Youme et al.'s UAV detector in Senegal ([Youme et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2021.05.037)), manual image interpretation in Nelson Mandela Bay ([Swanepoel and Marlin](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rsase.2024.101302)), the Dakar susceptibility map and Knoblauch et al.'s UAV model. Street-level work is newer still. Dabholkar et al. (2017) is the earliest verified "smart illegal dumping detection" paper, using fixed cameras ([Dabholkar et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/BigDataService.2017.51)), and 360° street-view mapping of 41 Dar es Salaam wards exists only as a conference talk ([State of the Map Africa](https://pretalx.com/state-of-the-map-africa-2025/talk/CN8JDN)).

## Smartphone fixes land within 5–13 metres, but the accuracy radius is a 1σ estimate

Smartphone positioning is accurate enough for crews to find a visible dump, and the evidence is consistent across a decade. Zandbergen and Barbeau (2011) report a **static outdoor median horizontal error of 5.0–8.5 m**, never exceeding 30 m outdoors ([Zandbergen and Barbeau](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0373463311000051)). Merry and Bettinger (2019) find **7–13 m** in an urban, multipath-heavy setting, with no seasonal effect ([Merry and Bettinger](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0219890)). The danger lies in fallback sources. Zandbergen's 2009 iPhone tests give medians of **8 m for A-GPS, 74 m for Wi-Fi and 600 m for cellular** positioning ([Zandbergen, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9671.2009.01152.x)). A phone indoors or with GPS off can therefore silently report a position hundreds of metres out. Dual-frequency (L1/L5) phones since the 2018 Xiaomi Mi 8 have lower code noise ([Robustelli et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/s21062125)). Tree cover degrades accuracy, which matters at vegetated peri-urban dump sites ([Lee et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0283090)).

The W3C Geolocation specification changes how the dashboard should draw positions. The November 2026 Candidate Recommendation Draft defines `accuracy` at the **68% confidence level (one standard deviation)**, whereas MDN and earlier wording said 95%. It also requires a secure context and express user permission, and treats `enableHighAccuracy` as a hint the browser may ignore ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)). Roughly one in three true positions therefore falls outside the drawn circle. Drawing a 2σ circle and flagging reports above about 50 m are defensible design choices. The design decision to attach live browser geolocation at capture time, rather than read EXIF afterwards, is also supported. Zielstra and Hochmair found that post-hoc photo geotags vary in accuracy by platform (Panoramio beat Flickr across 1,433 images; [Zielstra and Hochmair](https://doi.org/10.1080/14498596.2013.801331)). Evidence that WhatsApp and Facebook strip EXIF GPS is grey literature, and it conflicts: WhatsApp reportedly keeps metadata when an image is sent "as document" ([fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/whatsapp-telegram-signal-photo-metadata-preservation/)). Positional error of 5–74 m is far smaller than typical kernel-density bandwidths, so hotspot maps stay robust even when individual pins are imprecise. That is an inference worth stating.

## Citizen reporting works only when councils close the loop

The citizen-sensing literature supplies the dissertation's strongest design argument: **responsiveness drives participation**. In a study of **399,364 FixMyStreet users**, a successful first report was associated with a **57% higher probability** of a second one ([Sjoberg et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/puar.12697)). The system's SMS alerts and status updates are therefore a participation mechanism, not a convenience feature. Participation is also uneven: NYC 311 contact rates vary with neighbourhood socio-economic characteristics, so report maps measure who reports as well as where problems are ([Minkoff](https://doi.org/10.1177/1078087415577796)). Hotspot maps built from reports alone will under-represent low-engagement suburbs. This argues for presenting the system as one input to council planning, not a complete census.

The VGI literature agrees that crowdsourced data can be fit for purpose if validation is designed in. Goodchild coined "citizens as sensors" ([Goodchild, 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10708-007-9111-y)). Haklay found OpenStreetMap positionally comparable to Ordnance Survey data but with uneven coverage, poorer in deprived areas ([Haklay](https://doi.org/10.1068/b35097)). Goodchild and Li proposed three quality-assurance routes: crowdsourcing, social gatekeeping and geographic rules ([Goodchild and Li](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.spasta.2012.03.002)). Kosmala et al. showed that volunteer data can match professional data when projects build in training, validation and bias correction ([Kosmala et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/fee.1436)). The YOLO11 check on each submitted photo can be framed as an **automated form of Goodchild and Li's quality assurance**, filtering non-waste and spam images before they reach council staff. That framing connects the computer-vision and information-systems halves of the dissertation. No peer-reviewed evaluation of any waste-reporting app in Zimbabwe or SSA was found, and none of Litterati, TrashOut or SeeClickFix.

## Zimbabwe's data protection law makes the operator a licensed data controller

A system that collects geotagged photos processes personal data: the reporter's identity and location, and any bystanders' faces or vehicle plates. Zimbabwe's **Cyber and Data Protection Act [Chapter 12:07]** (Act 5 of 2021, republished with a corrected title on 11 March 2022) designates **POTRAZ as the Data Protection Authority** under s.5 ([Veritas](https://www.veritaszim.net/sites/veritas_d/files/Cyber%20%26%20Data%20Protection%20Act%20Cap1207%20No%205%20of%202021%20gaz%202022-03-11.pdf)). Cite the March 2022 republication, because Veritas also hosts an earlier mis-titled version ("Data Protection Act, Cap 11:22"). **SI 155 of 2024** (13 September 2024) introduced data-controller licensing and mandatory data protection officers. Secondary sources disagree on the licensing threshold, and one reports **24-hour breach notification** to POTRAZ ([MISA Zimbabwe](https://zimbabwe.misa.org/2024/11/09/misa-zimbabwe-analysis-and-position-on-new-data-regulations); [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/a-quick-start-guide-to-zimbabwes-data-protection-regulations)). POTRAZ compliance inspections were announced to begin on **1 September 2026** ([MEA TechWatch](https://meatechwatch.com/2026/03/25/data-crackdown-looms-potraz-targets-unlicensed-data-handlers/)). The council or the operator would therefore almost certainly be a data controller that needs a licence.

The privacy-engineering precedent is Google Street View's automated face and plate blurring, which tuned detection to favour recall (about 89% face recall *(verify)*; [Frome et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCV.2009.5459413)). Blurring before storage, stripping or coarsening EXIF data, and retaining only the capture-time location are defensible privacy-by-design measures. Alharbi et al.'s littering system, which detects faces and plates by design ([Alharbi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-77118-x)), is a useful counter-example of surveillance creep to discuss. The specific section numbers of the Act on sensitive data, security, breach and cross-border transfer were **not read from the primary text** in this research. Check them against the Veritas PDF before citing.

## DSR frames the study, CRISP-DM shapes the pipeline and Scrum delivers the software

The methodology sources support a three-layer structure. Design Science Research is the outer frame because the outputs are IT artefacts: a trained model and a reporting platform ([Hevner et al.](https://doi.org/10.2307/25148625)). Peffers et al.'s six-activity DSRM gives the chapter structure ([Peffers et al.](https://doi.org/10.2753/MIS0742-1222240302)). Gregor and Hevner's knowledge-contribution framework positions the work most plausibly as **"exaptation"**: known YOLO detectors applied to a new domain and context, citizen geotagged images in Zimbabwe ([Gregor and Hevner](https://doi.org/10.25300/MISQ/2013/37.2.01)). FEDS justifies artificial, summative evaluation (test-set mAP) followed by a naturalistic pilot or usability study ([Venable et al.](https://doi.org/10.1057/ejis.2014.36)). Inside the "design and development" activity, CRISP-DM structures the ML pipeline, and Schröer et al.'s systematic review shows it remains the most-used model and is routinely adapted ([Schröer et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.procs.2021.01.199)). Amershi et al.'s nine-stage Microsoft workflow justifies treating data labelling as a first-class stage and combining ML work with Agile delivery ([Amershi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE-SEIP.2019.00042)).

Two sources raise rigour expectations the dissertation should meet. Kapoor and Narayanan document leakage across ML-based science ([Kapoor and Narayanan](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804)), which for this project means splitting by site or capture session, not at random. Pineau et al.'s NeurIPS reproducibility checklist covers seeds, hyperparameters, compute and repeated runs ([Pineau et al.](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-303.html)). For the apps, SUS with Bangor et al.'s interpretation benchmarks gives a cheap, validated usability score ([Bangor et al.](https://doi.org/10.1080/10447310802205776)). ISO/IEC 25010:2023 can structure non-functional testing ([ISO](https://committee.iso.org/standard/78176.html)). Sassa et al. (2023), the proposal's Scrum source, is a descriptive review that **does not show Scrum suits ML projects**. Cite the Scrum Guide for the method and Amershi et al. for Agile-with-ML. Saunders' "research onion" fits poorly; if used at all, use it as a business-research contrast to DSR.

## Agreements, contradictions and the gaps the dissertation can claim

Across themes, the sources agree on five points. SSA collection is low and open dumping dominates. Zimbabwean waste failures are driven by municipal incapacity and infrequent collection. One-stage detectors with multi-scale necks, pre-training and augmentation are the efficient default. Real-world waste detection scores far below clean-background classification. Smartphone positioning within about 5–13 m is good enough for municipal action, and crowdsourced data is usable when validation is built in.

Several contradictions need explicit handling in the text. The first is collection coverage: **44% versus 31% versus about 55%** across different vintages. The second is Harare generation, 762 versus about 1,000 t/day, and fleet counts (15 compactors in one report, 48 compactors about half operational in a later one; [Herald](https://www.heraldonline.co.zw/harare-chi-town-choke-on-own-garbage/)). The third is the 2023–24 cholera deaths, 719 versus 718. The fourth and most consequential is causation: WHO attributes the 2018 outbreak to groundwater, while local media blame garbage. On the computer-vision side, "DETRs beat YOLOs" and YOLOv10's "1.8× faster than RT-DETR" are both true under their own benchmarking conditions, which shows that headline fps cannot be compared across papers. The W3C `accuracy` confidence level changed from 95% to 68%. Grey literature on WhatsApp EXIF stripping conflicts by send mode. Two characterisations of reference 1 also differ slightly. One verification pass reads it as general YOLO garbage detection; the full abstract shows it is a bin-fullness task. The narrower reading is the safer one.

The gaps the dissertation can legitimately claim are specific and well evidenced:

## Conclusion

The literature does more than tolerate this dissertation: its gaps define it. Overhead methods are accurate but expensive, infrequent and blind to fresh, small or occluded dumps. Ground-level datasets exist but none comes from Africa. Citizen-reporting platforms work but have never been evaluated in Zimbabwe. The project's distinctive contribution is the combination of all three: geotagged citizen photos, automated visual verification, and a council feedback loop in Harare and Bulawayo. That combination is best framed as DSR exaptation, and its YOLO filter doubles as the machine quality-assurance layer the VGI literature calls for.

The more uncomfortable implication is that the dissertation's most defensible metric is not the one YOLO papers lead with. Box mAP on amorphous piles is structurally depressed by ambiguous boundaries, merged-source label noise and domain shift. A photo-level precision and recall, measured on Zimbabwe-only test images split by site and including negatives, is both the operationally meaningful number and the one the literature has not yet standardised. Reporting it rigorously, alongside a YOLO26 comparison and a background-ratio ablation, would turn three current weaknesses into original methodological contributions.
