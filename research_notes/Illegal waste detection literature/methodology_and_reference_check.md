## Part A1: Design Science Research (DSR) as the overarching paradigm

### Takeaway
All four DSR sources exist with the details below. Together they give a defensible framing: the student builds an IT artefact (model plus reporting system), and evaluates it, which is exactly the DSR build/evaluate cycle. Peffers' six-step DSRM can structure the chapter, and FEDS can justify a mostly *ex post*, artificial evaluation (test-set metrics) followed by a small naturalistic evaluation (pilot or usability test).

### Cited Findings (annotated bibliography, Harvard)
- **Hevner, A.R., March, S.T., Park, J. and Ram, S. (2004) 'Design science in information systems research', *MIS Quarterly*, 28(1), pp. 75–106. doi:10.2307/25148625.** VERIFIED (Crossref). It sets out the founding DSR position (IT artefacts as research outputs) and seven guidelines: design as an artefact, problem relevance, design evaluation, research contributions, research rigour, design as a search process, and communication. *Use:* justify treating the YOLO11 model and reporting platform as the research contribution, and map the chapter onto the seven guidelines. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/25148625)
- **Peffers, K., Tuunanen, T., Rothenberger, M.A. and Chatterjee, S. (2007) 'A design science research methodology for information systems research', *Journal of Management Information Systems*, 24(3), pp. 45–77. doi:10.2753/MIS0742-1222240302.** VERIFIED (Crossref). It proposes the six-activity DSRM process: problem identification and motivation, objectives of a solution, design and development, demonstration, evaluation, and communication. *Use:* a ready-made chapter structure; Agile sprints and the CRISP-DM/ML pipeline sit inside the "design and development" activity. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2753/MIS0742-1222240302)
- **Gregor, S. and Hevner, A.R. (2013) 'Positioning and presenting design science research for maximum impact', *MIS Quarterly*, 37(2), pp. 337–355. doi:10.25300/MISQ/2013/37.2.01.** VERIFIED (Crossref). It gives the knowledge-contribution framework, a 2x2 of solution maturity and application-domain maturity with four quadrants: improvement, invention, routine design and exaptation. *Use:* position the contribution, most plausibly as "exaptation" (known YOLO detectors applied to a new domain and context: citizen geotagged images in Zimbabwe) or "improvement". — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.25300/MISQ/2013/37.2.01)
- **Venable, J., Pries-Heje, J. and Baskerville, R. (2016) 'FEDS: a Framework for Evaluation in Design Science Research', *European Journal of Information Systems*, 25(1), pp. 77–89. doi:10.1057/ejis.2014.36.** VERIFIED (Crossref). It offers a two-dimensional framework (formative vs summative, artificial vs naturalistic) and named evaluation strategies such as "Quick & Simple" and "Technical Risk & Efficacy". *Use:* justify the evaluation design, with artificial and summative ML metrics (mAP, precision, recall) plus formative evaluation during sprints and a naturalistic usability or pilot evaluation of the apps. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1057/ejis.2014.36)

### Inferences
- DSR fits better than Saunders' research onion because the output is an artefact, not a test of a business-management theory. Positivism/pragmatism can be named as the philosophical stance: a pragmatist DSR stance with positivist, quantitative evaluation of the model.

### Gaps
- None for existence. The four-quadrant names and the FEDS strategy names come from my knowledge of the papers. Abstracts were not fetched, so the student should confirm wording against the PDFs.

## Part A4: ML evaluation rigour, reproducibility and research philosophy

### Takeaway
Kapoor & Narayanan (2023) and Pineau et al. (2021) are verified, high-quality sources for justifying leakage-free splits (for example, no near-duplicate or same-site images across train, validation and test) and a reproducibility checklist (seeds, configs, released weights). Saunders et al. now has a 9th edition (2023).

### Cited Findings
- **Kapoor, S. and Narayanan, A. (2023) 'Leakage and the reproducibility crisis in machine-learning-based science', *Patterns*, 4(9), 100804. doi:10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804.** VERIFIED (Crossref). It documents data leakage across many ML-based science papers in multiple fields and proposes a leakage taxonomy and "model info sheets". *Use:* justify splitting by location or capture session rather than at random, deduplicating augmented or near-identical images before splitting, and keeping the test set untouched. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804)
- **Pineau, J., Vincent-Lamarre, P., Sinha, K., Larivière, V., Beygelzimer, A., d'Alché-Buc, F., Fox, E. and Larochelle, H. (2021) 'Improving reproducibility in machine learning research (a report from the NeurIPS 2019 Reproducibility Program)', *Journal of Machine Learning Research*, 22(164), pp. 1–20.** No DOI (JMLR). VERIFIED (JMLR page). It reports on the NeurIPS 2019 code-submission policy, the reproducibility challenge and the ML reproducibility checklist. *Use:* adopt the checklist (dataset statistics, hyperparameters, seeds, compute, number of runs, error bars). — [JMLR](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-303.html)
- **Saunders, M.N.K., Lewis, P. and Thornhill, A. (2023) *Research methods for business students*. 9th edn. Harlow: Pearson. ISBN 9781292402727.** Partly verified (retailer and Pearson listings). Pearson's own page shows a 2024/©2025 date for some formats, so cite the date printed in the copy used. The 8th edition is 2019. It is the source of the "research onion". *Use:* only as a contrast. Explain that the onion is a business-research heuristic and that DSR is the more appropriate frame for artefact construction in computing. — [Pearson](https://www.pearson.com/en-gb/subject-catalog/p/research-methods-for-business-students/P200000010080/9781292402727); [The Nile listing](https://www.thenile.co.nz/books/mark-saunders/research-methods-for-business-students/9781292402727)

### Inferences
- For a YOLO11 project, the leakage concern is concrete. Images from the same dump site or the same smartphone burst landing in both train and test will inflate mAP.

### Gaps
- No positivism-in-IS source was fetched. Goldkuhl (2012) above covers pragmatism. A classic positivist IS citation (for example Orlikowski & Baroudi 1991, *Information Systems Research* 2(1)) was not verified in this session.
