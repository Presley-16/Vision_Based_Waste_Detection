## Aerial, satellite and UAV detection of illegal dumping and landfills (2019–2026), including African examples

### Takeaway
Deep-learning detection from aerial, satellite and UAV images is now feasible at territorial scale. The best-documented result is ResNet50+FPN scene classification at 88% precision and 87% recall on 20 cm aerial imagery in Lombardy, Italy. The field is still held back by a lack of public datasets (AerialWaste was the first, with 3,478 positive samples), by image cost, resolution and revisit limits, and by the fact that these methods find large or long-standing sites rather than freshly dumped bags. That leaves room for ground-level, citizen-captured, geolocated photos, which is the niche this dissertation's system occupies.

### Cited Findings

(2021)** "Learning to identify illegal landfills through scene classification in aerial images", *Remote Sensing*, 13(22), 4520. doi:10.3390/rs13224520 **[VERIFIED]**. Treats landfill detection as multi-scale scene classification on a dataset of about 3,000 aerial images at 20 cm/pixel, labelled by expert photo interpreters. ResNet50 combined with a Feature Pyramid Network reached **88% precision and 87% recall** in a test area. ### Inferences
- Reported aerial results (88% precision and 87% recall in Torres & Fraternali 2021) depend on 20 cm airborne or VHR commercial imagery (WorldView-3, Pléiades). These sources cost money and are captured infrequently. None of the verified abstracts reports a revisit rate that would catch new dumping within days. Freely available Sentinel-2 (10 m) supports only susceptibility mapping (Scharf et al. 2025), not object detection.
- Aerial and satellite views from above cannot see waste under tree canopy, under bridges or in narrow alleys. This occlusion argument supports ground-level smartphone capture as a complement. UAVs (Kraft et al. 2021; Youme et al. 2021) close the resolution gap but bring flight-permission, operator and battery limits.
- African evidence is thin and mostly non-deep-learning or susceptibility-based (Dakar, Nelson Mandela Bay). Youme et al. (2021) is the main deep-learning example from Africa.

### Gaps
- Exact accuracy figures for Padubidri et al. (2022), Youme et al. (2021), Đidelija et al. (2022) and Kraft et al. (2021) need to be read from the full texts. The abstracts were missing or had no numbers.
- No verified source gave image acquisition costs (e.g., cost per km² of WorldView-3) or revisit intervals specific to dumping detection.
- El-Magd et al. (2022) could not be identified.

## Street-level imagery for waste and urban decay (Google Street View, dashcams, vehicle cameras)

### Takeaway
Street-level work is newer (mostly 2022–2025) and less well established than aerial work. Several 2025 papers combine street-view imagery with LLMs or multimodal deep learning to flag dumpsites, but I could not verify any with a DOI within this session's budget. The earliest verified "smart illegal dumping detection" paper uses fixed cameras, not Street View.

### Cited Findings
- **Dabholkar, A., Muthiyan, B., Srinivasan, S., Ravi, S., Jeon, H. and Gao, J. (2017)** "Smart illegal dumping detection", *2017 IEEE Third International Conference on Big Data Computing Service and Applications (BigDataService)*, pp. 255–260. doi:10.1109/BigDataService.2017.51 **[VERIFIED]** (bibliographic data only). An early deep-learning approach to detecting dumping in camera imagery. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/bigdataservice.2017.51)
- A 2025 *Waste Management* paper (Hong Kong) used LLM-extracted eye-level cues from street views plus census data to model illegal-dumping risk, reaching **R² = 0.7574** on held-out data. It found that compact hotspot clusters with visible waste or dense vegetation raise risk. **UNVERIFIED** (authors and DOI not confirmed; seen only via a repository listing). — [HKU Hub](https://hub.hku.hk/handle/10722/362232)
- "MultiSense DumpSpotter" (*Resources, Conservation and Recycling*, 2025): a multimodal deep-learning and LLM framework for street-level improper dumpsites. **UNVERIFIED** (authors and DOI not confirmed). — [HKBU Scholars](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/monitoring-street-level-improper-dumpsites-via-a-multi-modal-and-/)
- Dar es Salaam, Tanzania: visible municipal solid waste was mapped from 360° street-view imagery across **41 wards** (State of the Map Africa 2025 talk; not peer-reviewed). — [pretalx](https://pretalx.com/state-of-the-map-africa-2025/talk/CN8JDN)

### Inferences
- Street View and vehicle-mounted cameras give ground-level views but still have revisit gaps: Google Street View is updated every few years in many African cities, which is an inference that needs a citation. Citizen smartphone capture removes that latency, so the dissertation can position its system as "street-level imagery with continuous, crowd-driven revisit".

### Gaps
- No verified peer-reviewed Google Street View litter or dumping paper with a DOI was obtained. Suggested follow-up searches: "street view" "illegal dumping" in *Waste Management* 2025, and Pham, Nguyen and Donan (2022) on road-damage and litter detection with Street View (snippet only).
- No dashcam-based dumping detection paper was verified.

## Smartphone positioning accuracy (GPS/A-GPS, Wi-Fi, cellular, dual-frequency) and the W3C Geolocation API

### Takeaway
Single-frequency smartphone A-GPS gives a median horizontal error of about **5–8.5 m outdoors** and about **7–13 m in urban, multipath-heavy settings**. Wi-Fi-only fixes are roughly an order of magnitude worse (median **74 m**) and cell-only fixes worse again (median **600 m**), based on 2009 iPhone data. Dual-frequency (L1/L5) chips since 2018 reduce code noise. The W3C Geolocation API reports this uncertainty as `accuracy` in metres. The current draft (Nov 2026) defines it at a **68% (1σ)** confidence level, so a reported radius is not a guaranteed bound. That is directly relevant to how the dashboard should display report locations.

### Cited Findings
- **Zandbergen, P.A. (2009)** "Accuracy of iPhone locations: A comparison of assisted GPS, WiFi and cellular positioning", *Transactions in GIS*, 13(s1), pp. 5–25. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9671.2009.01152.x **[VERIFIED]**. On the 3G iPhone, the **A-GPS median error was 8 m** (ten 20-minute tests), worse than an autonomous GPS unit. **Wi-Fi median error was 74 m** (n = 58), which failed the published specification and showed erratic spatial patterns caused by how the Wi-Fi database was calibrated. **Cellular median error was 600 m** (n = 64). — [Wiley](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9671.2009.01152.x)
- **Zandbergen, P.A. and Barbeau, S.J. (2011)** "Positional accuracy of assisted GPS data from high-sensitivity GPS-enabled mobile phones", *Journal of Navigation*, 64(3), pp. 381–399. doi:10.1017/S0373463311000051 **[VERIFIED]**. Fix availability was close to 100% outdoors and indoors. **Static outdoor median horizontal error was 5.0–8.5 m**, 2–3 times worse than recreational GPS. **Maximum error never exceeded 30 m outdoors or 100 m indoors.** — [Cambridge](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0373463311000051)
- **Zandbergen, P.A. (2012)** "Comparison of WiFi positioning on two mobile devices", *Journal of Location Based Services*, 6(1), pp. 35–50. doi:10.1080/17489725.2011.630038 **[VERIFIED]** (bibliographic data only). — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/17489725.2011.630038)
- **Merry, K. and Bettinger, P. (2019)** "Smartphone GPS accuracy study in an urban environment", *PLOS ONE*, 14(7), e0219890. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0219890 **[VERIFIED]**. An iPhone 6 running Avenza, tested across 2 seasons, 2 times of day and with Wi-Fi on or off. **Average horizontal accuracy was 7–13 m**, comparable to recreation-grade receivers under heavy multipath. Season did not change average error, with or without Wi-Fi. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6638960/)
- **Lee, T., Bettinger, P., Merry, K. and Cieszewski, C. Compares a smartphone with Garmin and Trimble handhelds at 26 control points. Vegetation-induced multipath degrades accuracy, which is relevant to vegetated or peri-urban dump sites. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1371/journal.pone.0283090)
- **Menard, T., Miller, J., Nowak, M. and Norris, D. A companion paper also exists: Menard & Miller (2011), IEEE IV, pp. 278–283, doi:10.1109/IVS.2011.5940500. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/itsc.2011.6083141)
- **van Diggelen, F. and Enge, P. (2015)** "The world's first GPS MOOC and worldwide laboratory using smartphones", *Proceedings of ION GNSS+ 2015*, Tampa, FL, 14–18 Sept, pp. 361–369. **[VERIFIED – no DOI]** (listed in the ION GNSS+ 2015 proceedings table of contents; ION papers usually have no Crossref DOI). - **Robustelli, U., Paziewski, J. and Pugliano, G. - **Paziewski, J., Fortunato, M., Mazzoni, A. and Odolinski, R. - **W3C (2026)** *Geolocation*, W3C Candidate Recommendation Draft, 5 November 2026, eds. M. Cáceres and R. Grant. https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/ **[VERIFIED]**. `accuracy` is "a non-negative double that represents the accuracy value indicating the **68% confidence level in meters (1 standard deviation)**". The changelog records the change to 68%. MDN and older versions of the spec used **95%**; that older wording was not re-checked this session, so cite whichever version you accessed. `enableHighAccuracy` is only a hint the user agent may ignore, and it costs power and latency. `maximumAge` allows cached positions. The API works only in a **secure context** (an insecure origin gets `PERMISSION_DENIED`) and needs **express user permission**. The spec recommends limiting the permission to a single session. Spec status: it "returned to Candidate Recommendation" in March 2026. — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)

### Inferences
- When the browser fuses GPS, Wi-Fi and cell data, it reports whichever source wins. A phone indoors or with GPS off may return a Wi-Fi or cell fix of tens to hundreds of metres (74 m and 600 m medians in Zandbergen 2009) with a correspondingly large `accuracy`. The system should store `accuracy` and either reject or flag reports above a threshold (e.g., >50 m) and draw the accuracy circle on the Leaflet map.
- Because `accuracy` is now 1σ (68%), roughly 1 in 3 true positions falls outside the drawn circle. A 2σ display (×2) is the defensible choice for dispatching crews.
- Even the old 2009–2011 figures (5–8.5 m median) are enough for a crew to find a visible dump, so position error is less of a risk than spoofing or permission refusal.

### Gaps
- No verified 2020+ source gave an Android Fused Location Provider or browser-level accuracy figure (as opposed to raw GNSS).
- The urban-canyon versus open-sky numbers from van Diggelen & Enge (2015) were not confirmed.
- No verified source compared the reported `accuracy` with true error (calibration of the accuracy radius). This is a research gap the dissertation could test empirically.

## Photo geotag/EXIF reliability, metadata stripping and location spoofing

### Takeaway
Geotags written into photos after the fact are positionally unreliable: Panoramio geotags beat Flickr's in a 1,433-image, 45-area study. Messaging and social platforms often strip EXIF GPS, but the evidence for this is mostly grey literature. A peer-reviewed, DOI-bearing stripping study was not verified this session. This supports the design choice of attaching live browser geolocation at capture time. Live geolocation can still be spoofed through mock-location tools, and the peer-reviewed evidence on that was also not verified.

### Cited Findings
- **Zielstra, D. and Hochmair, H.H. (2013)** "Positional accuracy analysis of Flickr and Panoramio images for selected world regions", *Journal of Spatial Science*, 58(2), pp. 251–273. doi:10.1080/14498596.2013.801331 **[VERIFIED]**. Compared geotags of **1,433 images in 45 areas in 4 world regions** against manually corrected camera positions. **Panoramio was more accurate than Flickr**, with effects from image category and region. The median metre error was not retrieved. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/14498596.2013.801331)
- **Hauff, C. (2013)** "A study on the accuracy of Flickr's geotag data", *Proceedings of the 36th International ACM SIGIR Conference*, pp. 1037–1040. doi:10.1145/2484028.2484154 **[VERIFIED]** (bibliographic data only; no abstract or figures retrieved). — [TU Delft Pure](https://pure.tudelft.nl/portal/en/publications/a-study-on-the-accuracy-of-flickrs-geotag-data(d7178cce-baa7-48cd-ab93-00cae0ba2484).html)
- **Narain, S., Vo-Huu, T.D., Block, K. and Noubir, G. (2016)** "Inferring user routes and locations using zero-permission mobile sensors", *2016 IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy*, pp. 397–413. doi:10.1109/SP.2016.31 **[VERIFIED]** (bibliographic data only). Shows that a phone's location can leak through non-location sensors. This is a privacy rather than spoofing source, useful for an ethics or permissions discussion. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109/sp.2016.31)
- **Soni, N. (2025)** "Forensic value of Exif data: An analytical evaluation of metadata integrity across image transfer methods", *Perspectives in Legal and Forensic Sciences*, 2, 10006. doi:10.70322/plfs.2025.10006. Found via Crossref (the DOI resolves to this title), but the content and the journal's standing were **not checked**, so treat it as VERIFIED (existence only). — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.70322/plfs.2025.10006)
- IPTC (2013) industry test of **15 sites**: Facebook and Flickr were among "the worst offenders" for removing metadata. This is not peer-reviewed; cite it as grey literature. — [1854 Photography report](https://www.1854.photography/2013/03/study-exposes-social-media-sites-that-delete-photographs-metadata/)
- Vendor and forum tests (2025–2026, not peer-reviewed and conflicting) say WhatsApp strips EXIF and GPS in normal photo mode but keeps them when the image is sent "as document". They also say Facebook and X strip GPS on upload, although X uploads through the API may keep it. — [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/whatsapp-telegram-signal-photo-metadata-preservation/); [scanly](https://scanly.co/blog/social-media-exif-stripping)

### Inferences
- Reading EXIF GPS afterwards fails in three ways: the camera app may never write it (location tagging off), messaging and social apps strip it, and it can be edited trivially. Capture-time browser geolocation avoids the first two, but it needs a permission prompt and HTTPS (W3C 2026).
- Spoofing remains a threat to both approaches: Android developer-options mock location apps and browser devtools can override coordinates. Mitigations the dissertation can discuss include cross-checking with IP geolocation, flagging implausible `accuracy` values (e.g., exactly 0 or constant), checking movement plausibility between reports, and comparing EXIF and live location when both are present.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed DOI-bearing study of EXIF stripping by WhatsApp or Facebook was verified. Candidate to check: Castiglione, Cattaneo and De Santis (2011), "A forensic analysis of images on online social networks", INCoS 2011, believed to be doi:10.1109/INCoS.2011.43, **UNVERIFIED**.
- No peer-reviewed smartphone location-spoofing detection study for crowdsourcing was verified. Crossref returned only RF-level GNSS spoofing papers, such as Wang et al. (2025) in *GPS Solutions*, doi:10.1007/s10291-025-01996-5, which addresses a different threat.

## GIS/web mapping for municipal waste management, hotspot analysis and Leaflet/OpenStreetMap

### Takeaway
Web-GIS for municipal solid waste is well established, mostly for optimising collection. GIS susceptibility and hotspot mapping of illegal dumping is emerging (Dakar 2025, Nelson Mandela Bay 2024). I found no verified peer-reviewed paper that combines a Leaflet/OSM dashboard with citizen-geolocated dumping reports and SMS alerts. That combination is a gap the dissertation fills.

### Inferences
- Kernel density estimation (KDE) of report points on the dashboard is a natural extension. The positional error of 5–13 m GPS or 74 m Wi-Fi (above) is far smaller than typical KDE bandwidths (hundreds of metres), so hotspot maps are robust to smartphone error even when individual pins are not.

## Agreements, contradictions and gaps across the literature (for the Discussion chapter)

### Takeaway
Both strands agree that location and detection accuracy is good enough for municipal action. The real limits are cost and revisit frequency for aerial methods, and permissions, spoofing and metadata loss for smartphone geotags. Capture-time geolocation with a reported accuracy radius sits between the two strands, and the reliability of that radius has not been empirically studied.

### Cited Findings
- **Agreement on smartphone accuracy:** median errors of 5–8.5 m (Zandbergen & Barbeau 2011), 8 m (Zandbergen 2009) and 7–13 m urban (Merry & Bettinger 2019) are consistent across a decade, with multipath as the main degrader. Lee et al. (2023) find the same for tree cover. — sources above
- **Agreement on aerial data scarcity:** Torres & Fraternali (2023) state that no public landfill dataset existed before AerialWaste. Lin et al. (2024) build another dataset for the same reason. — sources above
- **Tension or contradiction:** the W3C `accuracy` confidence level is **68% (1σ)** in the 2026 draft but **95%** in MDN and earlier wording, so studies that interpret the radius need to state which version they used. — [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)
- **Tension or contradiction:** whether WhatsApp strips EXIF depends on the send mode (photo vs document) in the grey literature, so blanket claims that "messaging apps strip EXIF" are oversimplified. — [fast.io](https://fast.io/resources/whatsapp-telegram-signal-photo-metadata-preservation/)

### Inferences
- **Cost:** aerial and satellite detection needs VHR imagery and expert annotation (Torres & Fraternali 2021, 2023). Citizen smartphones shift that cost onto existing devices, but the system then depends on participation.
- **Revisit and latency:** aerial surveys are periodic, while citizen reports arrive in near real time. Satellite and aerial data suit audits of large or long-standing sites, and smartphone reports suit fresh dumping.
- **Occlusion:** views from above miss waste under canopy or structures, and ground-level photos miss dumping on inaccessible land. The two approaches complement each other.
- **Permissions and trust:** browser geolocation needs HTTPS and consent each session (W3C 2026). UAV flights need aviation permissions. Both affect how the system can be deployed.

### Gaps
- No verified study measured the latency from dumping to detection for any method.
- No verified study evaluated a citizen dumping-reporting app's location accuracy end to end.
